04 / Video-based climbing analysis

From climbing video to interpretable movement analysis.

The monocular-video research pipeline connects hold detection, human pose estimation and spatial mapping to trajectory, velocity and joint-angle analysis.

MY ROLE

Pipeline design and implementation; visual-perception integration, spatial mapping, movement analysis and evaluation.

CONTEXT

Master’s research · Robotic Systems Engineering · RWTH Aachen University.

Functional overviewMy contributionConcept illustrationEngineering outcome
01 / Project scope

Connect the observations to the quantities being analysed.

Competition footage contains both athlete movement and changing camera framing. The research covers the video-to-analysis pipeline, placing observations in a common wall reference and evaluating the resulting movement features.

02 / Functional overview

The complete research pipeline.

Hold observations establish the wall reference. Pose observations describe the athlete. Spatial mapping connects them before movement features are analysed.

03 / My contribution

Visual perception, spatial mapping and analysis as one system.

Pipeline design & visual perception

Prepared the video data and connected hold detection and human pose estimation to the downstream analysis.

Developed

Frame alignment & coordinate mapping

Movement analysis

Experimental evaluation

04 / Engineering considerations

Use a common reference and interpret each output carefully.

Separate changing framing from movement

The camera window changes while wall holds remain fixed. A common wall reference makes observations across frames comparable.

Analyse the pipeline at several levels

Perception quality, mapping consistency and movement interpretation each affect the final result. The research evaluated those stages as connected parts of the system.

06 / Engineering outcome

Trajectory, velocity and joint-angle analysis from video.

The implemented workflow produces trajectories, velocity profiles and joint-angle analysis from climbing video. Mapping observations to a common reference makes them comparable across changing camera frames.

Technical boundaries

A wall reference and monocular pose estimates do not recover complete metric three-dimensional motion. Template dependence and depth ambiguity remain relevant to interpretation.

Functional views regroup responsibilities rather than reproduce internal implementation. Animations and illustrative geometry are independently created using synthetic data, not product recordings or measured results.
